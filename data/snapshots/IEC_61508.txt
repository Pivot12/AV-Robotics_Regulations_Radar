# Safety and functional safety

Safety is at the heart of IEC’s mission 

### From automatic doors to smoke detectors, we depend on technology for protection every day.  
 Electrical and electronic products bring huge benefits but can pose risks if not designed or operated correctly.  
 Electric shock, overheating or system failures can cause harm to people, property and the environment.

## What is functional safety?

Functional safety refers to the automatic responses that keep systems safe when something goes wrong. Examples include emergency shutdown systems in a chemical process plant, or systems that detect fires or toxic gas releases and automatically initiate safety actions.

These safeguards protect millions of people worldwide.

## Why it matters now

As systems become more connected, automated and software-driven, ensuring safety grows more complex. Advanced manufacturing, robotics, AI and the Internet of Things bring opportunities—along with new safety risks.

## The role of IEC Standards

International Standards help manufacturers and designers manage these risks. The globally recognized [IEC 61508 series](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/22273) offers a common framework for functional safety in electrical, electronic and programmable systems. It helps organizations:

*   Identify hazards 
*   Assess and manage risks 
*   Design reliable safety systems throughout a product’s lifecycle 

[Functional safety FAQ](https://www.iec.ch/functional-safety/faq)

IEC 61508 is the foundation for sector-specific foundational safety standards used in manufacturing, energy, transportation, healthcare and process automation.

[Learn more about how IEC 61508 can help your organization.](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5514)

### IEC’s commitment to safety

The **Advisory Committee on Safety** ([ACOS](https://www.iec.ch/dyn/www/f?p=103:41:0::::FSP_ORG_ID,FSP_LANG_ID:3235,25)) ensures that IEC standards and conformity assessment activities systematically address safety issues across all areas of electrotechnology.
